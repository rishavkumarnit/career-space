Design Splitwise

A low-level design for recording shared expenses, calculating equal, exact, and percentage splits, tracking net balances, and settling debts. The complete example runs as one Java file.

Functional requirements

  • Add users.
  • Record an expense paid by one user for multiple participants.
  • Support equal, exact, and percentage splits.
  • Show who owes whom and how much.
  • Net reverse debts automatically.
  • Allow partial or full settlement.
Money rule: Use BigDecimal, a fixed two-decimal scale, and an explicit rounding policy. Binary floating-point types such as double can introduce rounding errors.

Split strategies

The Strategy pattern isolates the calculation rules. Adding another method, such as shares or adjustments, does not require changing balance storage.

StrategyInputValidation
EqualParticipantsDivide total by count; give the final participant the rounding remainder.
ExactOne amount per participantAll amounts must sum to the expense total.
PercentOne percentage per participantPercentages must sum to 100; correct the final rounding remainder.

The factory maps SplitType to the correct strategy. The service calls only the common SplitStrategy.calculate() contract.

Balance and netting logic

The balance sheet stores a directed edge:

balances[creditor][debtor] = amount the debtor owes the creditor

When Rishav pays ₹600 for Aman, add an edge Rishav ← Aman: ₹600. If Aman later pays ₹250 for Rishav, subtract the reverse edge and keep only the net debt:

Rishav ← Aman: 600
Aman   ← Rishav: 250
----------------------
Rishav ← Aman: 350

This pairwise netting keeps output readable and avoids simultaneously storing opposite debts between two users. It does not globally minimize the number of transactions across a large group; that can be a separate simplification step.

Expense request flow

  1. ValidateCheck payer, participants, uniqueness, and positive total.
  2. Select strategyChoose equal, exact, or percent from the expense type.
  3. Calculate sharesProduce one monetary share per participant.
  4. Update balancesEvery participant except the payer owes their share to the payer.
  5. Save expenseKeep an immutable expense record for history and audit.

Topics to know for the interview

Object-oriented design

Entities, value objects, immutability, encapsulation, composition, and assigning each class one responsibility.

Strategy and Factory patterns

Strategy makes split rules interchangeable. Factory centralizes strategy selection. Discuss when dependency injection would replace direct construction.

Money and rounding

Explain why BigDecimal is safer than double, how scale and rounding mode work, and how to allocate the remainder without losing money.

Validation and invariants

Exact shares equal the total, percentages equal 100, participant IDs are valid and unique, totals are positive, and settlements never exceed current debt.

Concurrency and consistency

Two expenses may update the same balance concurrently. A production design needs database transactions, optimistic or pessimistic locking, idempotency keys, and a clear consistency model.

Persistence design

Separate users, groups, expenses, splits, and settlements. Balances can be computed from a ledger or maintained as a projection for faster reads.

Production extensions

  • 01Groups, group membership, and group-specific currencies.
  • 02Recurring expenses, comments, receipts, and notifications.
  • 03A ledger of immutable expense and settlement events for auditability.
  • 04Global debt simplification to reduce the number of payments.
  • 05REST APIs, authentication, authorization, pagination, and idempotency.
  • 06Multi-currency conversion with an exchange-rate snapshot per expense.
